Australian Senior Secondary Education

Subject Choices in Senior Secondary School

What students study across Australia, with a closer look at Queensland.

What do Year 12 students choose to study, and how have those choices changed over time? This visualisation begins with Australia wide data on participation across learning areas, gender and languages. It then takes a closer look at individual subjects across Queensland schools and education regions.

01

What are students choosing?

Australia wide data for 2024 shows which learning areas have the highest Year 12 participation, where male and female participation differs, and which languages are most commonly studied.

What stands out?

English leads at 79.5%, followed by Mathematics at 67.0%. Female participation is higher in six of the eight learning areas, while male participation is higher in Mathematics and Technology. Japanese, French and Chinese together account for 58.6% of Year 12 language enrolments.

Figures cover tertiary recognised Year 12 subjects. Students can be counted in more than one learning area, so the participation percentages do not add to 100%. The treemap shows each language's share of Year 12 language enrolments.

Source: ACARA - Year 12 Subject Enrolments

02

How have subject choices changed?

Subject choices change over time. Looking across several years shows which learning areas have gained or lost participation, how gender differences have changed, and how language rankings have shifted.

What stands out?

Participation is lower in 2024 than in 2010 across all eight learning areas. Arts records the largest decrease, falling from 29.2% to 20.7%, a drop of 8.5 percentage points. Female participation remains higher in Arts, while male participation remains higher in Technology and Mathematics. Japanese, French and Chinese remain the three highest ranked languages throughout the period.

The heatmap shows female participation minus male participation in percentage points (pp). Positive values mean higher female participation, while negative values mean higher male participation.

Source: ACARA - Year 12 Subject Enrolments

03

A closer look at Queensland

The data gives a broad view through learning areas. Queensland data allows a closer look at individual senior subjects, showing which subjects have the most Unit 4 enrolments and how male and female enrolments differ across selected subjects.

What stands out?

English has the most Unit 4 enrolments at 27,822, followed by General Mathematics at 21,381. Essential English and Essential Mathematics each have around 20,000 enrolments. Biology has more female than male enrolments, while Physics and Sport & Recreation have more male than female enrolments.

These figures show Unit 4 enrolments for the 2025 completion year. They count students enrolled in each subject, not students who passed. Students taking several subjects are counted once in each subject they study.

Source: QCAA - Senior Subject Enrolment Data

04

Where are subject choices concentrated in Queensland?

Large enrolment totals do not always mean a subject makes up a larger share of study within a region. These maps show where selected subjects are studied across Queensland and compare school locations, regional enrolment totals and subject shares. Choose a subject using the dropdown on each map.

What stands out?

Regional enrolment totals and subject shares do not always show the same pattern. A region can have a large number of enrolments in a subject without that subject making up a particularly large share of its overall Unit 4 enrolments.

Dots show school sites with recorded enrolments in the selected subject. Two schools without matched coordinates are not shown on the dot map. Education region boundaries are simplified, with offshore islands omitted and Norfolk Island excluded.

Subject share is calculated by dividing enrolments in the selected subject by all recorded Unit 4 subject enrolments in the region, then multiplying by 100. The total includes subjects outside the dropdown and measures enrolments rather than individual students. The colour scale changes with the selected subject.

Sources: QCAA - Senior Subject Enrolment Data by School ; Queensland Government - Education Region Boundaries ; Queensland Government - School Locations
